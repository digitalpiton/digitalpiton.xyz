Chapter II: Endless Struggle

11/14/26

Dedicated song: Headache - Truisms 4 Dummies

When I saw the words "endless struggle" recently, my first impression was to frame myself as a hero raging against the world, against the injustices of life itself. But that is only one interepretation for when a person is feeling a particular way, and it doesn't really hold up once your state of mind changes.

Another way someone could look at the words "endless struggle" like I am now is this feeling that you are struggling against some part of yourself, and that if you lose this struggle, you paradoxically guarantee it will persist further. It's like what people talk about where you have to be perfect every time, and this other part of you only has to get you to fail once for the whole struggle to be rendered pointless.

And that's where so many people are nowadays, some aware of it and some not, locked in this battle with themselves, facing a return to the drawing board should they ever fail and slip back into their old ways. There's something deeply unintutive to me about that, though. The asymmetry of that has always reminded me of when the IRA threatened Margaret Thatcher, telling her that she had to be lucky every time, while they only had to be lucky once. Like what, is this part of myself addicted to the internet a terrorist threatening to destroy the rest of myself? Has the internet created a hegemony in my brain where at any point, my best and most advanced faculties can be completely overridden and I am just charged with dealing with the consequences later?

The difference between a terrorist and the sovreign it's threatening and my mind though, is that my mind, the side I want to win, and the side I would relate to, doesn't actually have an agenda at all. People will get so caught up with talking about discipline and self-improvement, but most of the time they're just living out someone else's ideal. We can't all be ripped, focusing on our macros and looksmaxxing. We can't all spend our time reading Aristotle and Camus and expect it get anything from it. We can't all dabble in photography, and journaling, and having our Anti-Brainrot Everyday Carry (EDC)™. In other words, it doesn't seem to work to tell someone to have an agenda, or a reason for not falling head-first into doomscrolling and gooning.

When people try suggesting alternatives to others, it's in the right spirit, it's just the advice is often given too prescriptively and taken too literally. A person does need an "agenda". They do need some deep-seated, high-stakes reason to not do scroll on their phone all day. These companies have had decades to build their own agenda that involves us becoming like everyone else, encased into these exchangable economic units whose attention has properties that can be reliably measured and predicted. Accomplishing that involves making us all as much alike as possible to where we fit comfortably into a commercially-optimized digital paradigm.

And I don't mean to downplay the importance of discipline or self-improvement. These are necessary things, but they are as personal as individual talents are. What works for me almost certainly is not going to work for you, except maybe when it is expressed in the absolute broadest of terms. If you have lost what your talents are, what God-given opportunities you have to be exceptional, you have to go find those, and it has to be important to you. You're looking for something that takes less effort than most things, despite yielding more positive results. All three of those aspects, the effort, "most things", and positive results are all specific to the person, and cannot be translated or passed along from one to another through a YouTube video, TED Talk, or self-help podcast, at least that's what it appears like to me. We'll simply have to try something, and if it doesn't work, we'll have to try something else.

Awe

11/11/26

Dedicated song: Accelera Deck - This Bliss

If you're not from one, any city big enough to have skyscrapers is kind of a fascinating thing. Even driving through it is like what I estimate it must feel like to be an extra in some film that's going to be remembered as a once-in-a-lifetime magnum opus. Maybe it's also fascinating to people who are from one. It's easy to romanticize, and I assume if you had some place like this within 20 minutes, it would have been impossible to ever be bored. But obviously that's not true.

But I'm not really interested in equivocating like we do with ourselves, where, as soon as we try to be innocently romantic about life, and maybe rediscover some of the bliss we would experience as a kid, our 9-5 attitude kicks in and we have to weigh it down with a thousand caveats and self-defeating qualifiers that accomplish the opposite of what basically any human endeveavor is meant to do: make something better. It is magical going through some city with these glittering temples of commerce flanking you on both sides. It is as memorable a feeling as seeing mountains on the horizon. I've always felt reassured by things like this, the same as seeing the giant container ships come into port, and realizing that this happens every day.

Because the lives we're compelled to live sort of by default will usually enforce this implicit belief that change is impossible after a certain point. Like: you get one shot, and if you blow it, and you wake up and you're 24, and you're not happy with your life, it's over. Hang it up. Queue Pink Floyd - Time. But for me, stuff like the giant buildings, and mountains, and container ships challenge that. When I see them, I find it easy to relinquish the arrogance that says I know the rest of my life, and all of it will suck. Awe is a humbling thing.
